Accessible & Fair Practices

Places and project processes should be considered from the perspective of the people who use them, work on them and make decisions about them.

AI-generated conceptual illustration · Not a company project or team photograph.

Accessibility begins in the brief

Different people experience a place in different ways. Access routes, circulation, entrances, wayfinding and shared facilities should be considered early, with the applicable local requirements and specialist input. The aim is to identify barriers while there is still an opportunity to address them through design.

Fairness in the working process

Clear scopes, comparable information and transparent evaluation criteria make a project process easier to understand. In procurement and partner coordination, requirements and responsibilities should be stated consistently, with decisions recorded and changes communicated to the affected parties.

  • Use clear project requirements and role descriptions.
  • Provide an understandable route for questions and clarifications.
  • Document evaluation criteria, decisions and agreed changes.

Respect across project relationships

A constructive project environment depends on respectful communication and an understanding of each party’s role. Concerns about access, working conditions or coordination should be raised through the agreed project channels. Relevant statutory duties and contractual obligations remain specific to the location and appointment.
